The hardest design problem in B2B enterprise software is not the interface — it is the workflow that exists before the interface. Distributor sales reps have built habits and shortcuts over years inside systems that were never designed for them. Proton's job is to make AI-powered recommendations feel like a natural part of that existing motion, not a new thing to learn on top of it. That tension between legacy behavior and new capability is the kind of design problem I find worth spending real time on.

For the past four years I have designed complex B2B workflows from scratch — at FinchTrade and MarketGuard, both built for professional users with high-stakes, multi-step processes. At FinchTrade I redesigned the onboarding document workflow for a B2B liquidity provider; the redesign reduced activation time by 79% from v1, and the product website went from a 68% bounce rate to 14% in the months following. At MarketGuard — an AML/KYT compliance platform with separate portals for traders and compliance officers — the challenge was translating a hard regulatory requirement (a full AML case review) into a flow that users could complete without errors or abandonment. Both products are live at finchtrade.com and marketguard.io; the full process, including research artifacts, is in my portfolio at in-buro.com.

I also completed a 2.5-month AI Native Designer course focused specifically on agentic workflows in product design. Alongside that, I have built three design systems across separate products — token-based, with components, interactions, and documentation — and I maintain them across product evolution, not just as a launch deliverable.
